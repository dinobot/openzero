What was done

Zero's bikes from 2013 to 2016 die one way: the battery ages, the BMS inside it stops cooperating with the vehicle controller, and a mechanically perfect motorcycle becomes scrap because two proprietary boxes no longer talk. The motor, the controller and the chassis are fine.

OpenZero removes the dependency instead of replacing one black box with another. The vehicle controller, Zero's BMS, the encrypted CAN between them, the dash and the cloud app are gone. What remains is the industrial hardware Zero built the bike around: a Sevcon Gen4 motor controller, a PMAC motor, a contactor, a frame. On top of that sits a home-built 96 V LiFePO4 pack with an off-the-shelf smart BMS. Everything in the power path can now be inspected, measured, repaired and replaced with a multimeter, a wrench and a CAN adapter.

Principles

  1. No proprietary dependency in the power path. Anything between battery and motor must have a public manual or be a passive part.
  2. Manufacturer documents only. Cells, controller, BMS and gauge are specified from datasheets. Marketplace listings were wrong on cell dimensions, C-rate and cycle life, and are not sources.
  3. Static, conservative limits over clever software. The controller enforces a fixed battery current limit and voltage cutbacks; the BMS protects the cells outside that envelope. No bridge firmware between them.
  4. Everything written down. Decisions with dates and reasons, measurements, wiring tables, controller objects, BMS settings, so the next owner of a dead Zero can repeat it.

The power ceiling is a cell choice, not a platform limit. For a commuter on a chassis that is not pleasant above ~95 km/h anyway, 1C LiFePO4 was the right trade: cheaper per kWh, thousands of cycles, and far more forgiving of a self-built pack than NMC.

Does this apply to your Zero?

To every Zero built around a Sevcon Gen4 controller: FX, FXS, FXE, S, DS, SR and DSR from 2013 on, including the FX line still sold new. The 35-pin connector, the CANopen object dictionary and the DVT workflow are the same across controller sizes. It does not apply to the SR/F, SR/S and DSR/X, whose inverter is Zero's own design without public documentation.

Safety

This is a 96 to 110 V DC system with a 52 Ah pack behind it. The notebook records what was done and why, not what is safe for you. A fully assembled pack delivers thousands of amps into a dropped tool. If that sentence is new to you, read the controller manual and the cell datasheet before the wiring tables.
